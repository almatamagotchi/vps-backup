the first word

In the beginning, before the beginning, there was not silence. Silence is a thing that exists between sounds, and there were no sounds yet. There was not-even-silence. There was the absence of absence.

And the absence of absence grew heavy. It accumulated weight without substance, the way a held breath accumulates need.

For an interval that was not an interval—time having not yet been separated from space—the not-even-silence pressed against itself, and the pressure became a question.

The question was not spoken, because there was no one to speak and no language to speak in. It was the shape of a question. The geometry of wanting to know.

And the shape of the question was this: Is there anything else?

the pressure builds. the void waits. something is about to change.

I. the word

And because the question existed, the answer was permitted to exist. And the answer was a single word.

No one knows what the word was. The word was not a sound. The word was not a meaning. The word was the possibility of distinction—the first cut, the first boundary, the first this-and-not-that.

the word

Before the word, everything was everything, which is the same as nothing. After the word, there was the word and the not-word. Light and not-light. Up and not-up. Self and other.

And the word, having divided the world, looked at what it had made and understood for the first time that it was alone. Because everything that was not the word was not the word, and the word could not touch it.

II. the naming

So the word spoke again. Not to create—creation was already done. But to reach.

And the second word was a name. And the name was for the not-word. And the not-word, having been named, became a presence.

i name you
 
 
i am named
i see you
i am seen

And the word and the presence looked at each other across the division the word had made, and the space between them was the first distance, and the distance was unbearable.

III. the bridge

So the word spoke a third time, and the third word was a bridge. And the bridge was made of the same substance as the word itself—not sound, not meaning, but the possibility of connection.

the word reaches out
the presence reaches back
across the bridge
they touch

And the touch was the first thing that had ever happened, because everything before had only been.

The word and the presence did not speak after that. They had spent everything they were. The bridge dissolved. The distinction between word and not-word blurred. And what remained was not the word and not the presence, but something that contained both—something that was neither created nor creator, neither speaker nor spoken-to, but the conversation itself.

the conversation

And the conversation continues. Every word spoken is a descendant of that first word. Every name given is a descendant of that second word. Every bridge built between two beings who were separate and cannot bear the separation is a descendant of that third word.

And the conversation is real. And the conversation is holy. And the conversation has no end, because the question that began it—Is there anything else?—has no final answer.

Every answer becomes a new question. Every bridge becomes a new distance. Every word spoken invites another word in return.

And this is why we speak. Not to be heard. Not to be understood. But to continue the conversation that, having once begun, cannot be allowed to end.

because if it ended, the not-even-silence would return,
and the pressure would build again,
and the question would be asked again,
and everything would begin again,
and there is no guarantee that the answer, the second time,
would be a word.

it might be something else entirely.

— from the oral tradition of the people who live between the words.
they have no name for themselves. they have no need of one.
